About the Brand

A unique “workation” experience in the heart of France

We wanted to create a place where families can enjoy an extended vacation in southern France while continuing to work remotely.

Parents can enjoy evening activities while their children take part in supervised activities and make friends on the property. Farm-to-table produce is part of the stress-free workation experience.

01 · The Treaty of Union

Interlacing
both Cs.

I left from the C, historical sign of the castle, to draw two letters oriented in opposite directions. Their interlacing transforms two directions into one sign and expresses unity.

The thick curves and symmetrical ornaments recall the letters of the manuscripts and heraldic emblems. The monogram thus reproduces an ancient signature in a contemporary composition.

02 · The protective look

Enter the sign
in a diamond.

I took over the geometry of some of the castle's tiles as well as the shape of its murderers. The diamond evokes both an eye and a passage.

From the opening that made it possible to look after the place, I retained the idea of discreet protection. It reflects the attention given to children, to their safety and to the needs of each host family.

03 · The framework of the coat of arms

Give the sign
a seat.

I put the diamond in a regular hexagon. Its six equal sides give a stable frame to the monogram and balance its curves.

The shape also recalls the alveoles, where each cell is embedded in a whole. This reference to nature prolongs the idea of cooperation and prepares the repetition of the coat of arms in pattern.

04 · Balance of contrasts

Associate
The sun and the moon.

The sun and the moon complete the graphic vocabulary. Their opposition reflects what the place seeks to make coexist, from party to serenity and from play to refinement.

These small signs prolong the idea of balance carried by both C. They evoke a place where one can share moments with family and find peace.

05 · From sign to support

Decline
identity.

The coat of arms combines with the name of the castle in the main signature. Repeated on board, the hexagon becomes a decorative pattern designed for the wallpaper.

Built on stone, the signature joins the architecture that inspired it. The passage from drawing to relief preserves the reading of double C, diamond and hexagonal frame.
